About

My background is in structural health monitoring, nondestructive evaluation, and civil infrastructure. I develop methods that combine physical models, laboratory measurements, and machine learning. I’m particularly interested in how engineers can distinguish a real change in an asset from a change in the way it was measured.

Research areas

My work connects infrastructure monitoring with operational decisions across three systems.

  • Renewable energy systems

    I study how AI can support renewable hydrogen production and delivery, including electrolyzer dispatch, storage, leak response, and maintenance. I’m interested in measuring the effects on energy use, hydrogen loss, and emissions.

  • Water systems

    I work on sensor placement and data analysis for water, wastewater, and stormwater infrastructure. The aim is to decide where measurements are most useful and which assets need inspection.

  • Oil and gas systems

    I combine guided-wave measurements, laboratory tests, and simulations to detect pipeline defects and understand how materials degrade.

Selected projects

Three collaborations that connect research methods to infrastructure decisions.

  • Co-PI · U.S. DOT PHMSA

    Super-H₂: Risk assessment for hydrogen pipelines

    I served as a co-principal investigator on research assessing whether existing pipelines are suitable for hydrogen service. The project brings together risk assessment, mitigation measures, and decision-support methods.

    Read the public PHMSA project report
  • Co-PI · Water Research Foundation Project 5239

    Sensor networks for wastewater and stormwater

    Where should a utility place sensors to get the most useful information about its collection system? This project studies sensor layout, advanced sensing, and data analytics. I co-authored an overview that connects sensor placement to collection-system monitoring.

    View the WRF project
  • Pipeline integrity · Experimental research

    Understanding erosion at pipeline bends

    Our team studied how solid particles move through a pipe elbow and tested guide vanes in the lab. The work connects particle motion to where erosion develops and how the flow can be redirected.

    Read the AIP Scilight
